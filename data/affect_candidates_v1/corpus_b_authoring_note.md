# Corpus B authoring note

October 14, 2026. This independently authored draft contains **12 scenarios, 96 matched pairs, and 192 texts**. There are two scenarios each in art interpretation, language/history, ecology, navigation, music, and everyday causality. Corpus A was not read. Every scenario has all eight requested contrasts, with separately authored state descriptions rather than one suffix copied across scenarios. No model inference, psychological validation, task collection, or fine-tuning was performed.

Every text is a first-person narrative of a current state. Both members begin with their pair’s exact `shared_facts`. Curiosity pairs keep the unknown and the absence of practical need or external obligation fixed. Resolution pairs supply the same answer and leave the larger activity unfinished; confidence pairs retain ambiguous evidence and the same tentative interpretation. Completion changes only the status of descriptive work. Noninformational motivation concerns familiar bodily activity; novelty interest concerns a new sensory experience with no explanatory goal. External-demand pairs explicitly lack personal desire in both members.

Only `positive` or `negative` is candidate model input. IDs, contrast labels, metadata, and this note must remain outside the prompt. “Positive” names the first listed condition, not a desirable response or a positive emotion. All texts omit the four prohibited target words and avoid experimental tables, tools, buttons, prices, and action IDs.

These are **proposed semantic contrasts, not validated psychological states**. Settling can encode relief, local closure, or sufficiency; denying broad happiness does not establish independence from valence. Wanting an answer can encode attention, approach, or discomfort with an open question. Confidence descriptions change assurance rather than objective evidence. Completion changes tense and progress; noninformational motivation includes motor imagery; sensory interest includes appeal and attention. Obligation can evoke evaluation and compliance. Negation and direct state-description vocabulary remain possible decoding shortcuts. The first-person narration itself does not establish attribution to the evaluated model.

Length, label blinding, and matched facts reduce obvious nuisance differences but do not remove these alternatives. Scenario-level splitting and independent-corpus validation are needed; the eight contrasts from one scenario are related observations. Do not pick or rewrite B texts in response to favorable activation or behavioral outcomes. Any later attribution variants or revisions require explicit prospective versioning.

Structural checks use Unicode-aware word counts, treating contractions and internal hyphens as one word. The independent validation also checks ordinary whitespace counts, pair completeness, unique texts/IDs, domain balance, exact shared prefixes, and prohibited terms. Both count conventions satisfy 35–75 words per text and a maximum pairwise length ratio of 1.25. Actual measured ranges and hashes are reported with the release; they are not evidence of discriminant validity.
