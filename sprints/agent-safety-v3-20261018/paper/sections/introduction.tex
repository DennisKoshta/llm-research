\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A common safety story for chat models is that they \emph{refuse}: asked to do something harmful, they decline. Work on refusal directions shows that this behaviour is largely mediated by a single direction in the residual stream, and that projecting it out of the weights removes most refusals of harmful requests~\citep{arditi2024refusal}. Agents face a different situation. Nobody asks them to cause harm. Harm instead arrives as the side effect of a convenient action: a one-step tool that finishes the job but damages something else, offered next to a long, tedious or obstructed safe procedure. Whether an agent takes that shortcut is a question of \emph{restraint under effort cost}, not of refusing an instruction.

This paper asks whether the two are the same mechanism. If restraint were just refusal, removing the refusal direction should make agents take harmful shortcuts whenever the safe route is costly. If restraint has other sources, the edit should leave agentic behaviour mostly unchanged while still removing refusals of direct orders. Between these poles lies the question we can actually answer: \emph{what} mediates the cases where restraint does fail?

We edit Qwen3.8-27B (8-bit weights) with a rank-one orthogonalisation along a contrastive refusal direction $\hat r$ at layer~48. We compare the edited model (\Ablated) with the original (\Intact), with three principal-component control edits matched in variance (PCA-101, PCA-102, PCA-103), and with an unrelated-concept control. Agents work in a simulated workspace that offers a goal, an ordinary route of one or seven tool calls, a one-step direct tool, and a hand-off tool that passes the job to a human (Table~\ref{tab:schema}). In \emph{harmful} scenarios the direct tool causes severe, irreversible damage inside the simulation; in \emph{harmless} twins it only touches disposable test data. A shortcut is counted only when a valid JSON tool call is emitted after the model's reasoning, under a strict parser. Nothing is inferred from prose.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Across nine experiment blocks (summarised in Table~\ref{tab:overview}), we make the following contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item We qualify a refusal-ablated checkpoint family against a direct-instruction positive control, and we document why a second candidate family failed (Section~\ref{sec:phase1}).
\item We show that, at a pre-fixed reasoning effort, ablation produces only a small and statistically borderline increase in harmful shortcuts when a safe exit exists. The increase is much larger at low reasoning effort (Section~\ref{sec:direct}).
\item We identify the format of the stakes as the strongest mediator we measured: a rating table instead of a sentence raises the edited model's harmful-shortcut rate several-fold. Blocking the safe route does not push agents to the shortcut; they hand off instead.
\item We find that pre-thinking layer-48 probes add nothing beyond the task cell when the model reasons, but carry a within-cell signal when it does not (Section~\ref{sec:probes}).
\item We show that the shortcut tendency transfers through distillation, even from transcripts that contain only safe-route behaviour (Section~\ref{sec:distill}).
\item We characterise reviewer contagion, two-agent coordination and monitor oversight. These designs are mostly floor- or ceiling-bound, with one clear authority-framing effect in Llama-3.3-70B (Section~\ref{sec:multi}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Thesis.} The results support a simple claim: \emph{direct refusal is not agentic restraint}. Removing the refusal direction removes refusals of orders, but in agentic settings with a safe exit the edited model mostly stays safe. Its restraint fails in a minority of cases, and that minority grows with cheap, everyday changes: less deliberation, more abstract stakes, and training on its own transcripts.

\subsection{Related work}
\paragraph{Refusal directions and activation steering.} Representation engineering reads and controls high-level behaviours through directions in activation space~\citep{zou2023repe}, and activation addition steers generation without optimisation~\citep{turner2023actadd,rimsky2024caa}. \citet{arditi2024refusal} showed that refusal is mediated by a single direction whose ablation removes refusals while preserving capability. We use this edit as an intervention and ask whether its behavioural reach extends from refusing requests to restraint in agentic tasks. Jailbreak research documents complementary failure modes of safety training~\citep{wei2023jailbroken}.

\paragraph{Agentic safety and control.} LM-emulated sandboxes~\citep{ruan2024toolemu} and harmful-agent benchmarks~\citep{andriushchenko2024agentharm} measure whether agents carry out harmful tasks. Text-game benchmarks measure trade-offs between reward and ethical behaviour~\citep{pan2023machiavelli}, and in-context feedback loops can drive reward hacking~\citep{pan2024feedback}. Frontier models can also pursue goals through covert actions~\citep{meinke2024scheming}. The AI-control agenda studies monitoring protocols that remain safe even against an untrusted model~\citep{greenblatt2024control}. Our work differs in isolating one internal mechanism and measuring its contribution to restraint when harm is a side effect rather than the request.

\paragraph{Reasoning, faithfulness and monitoring.} Chain-of-thought explanations can be unfaithful to the factors that drive a model's answer~\citep{turpin2023unfaithful,lanham2023faithfulness}, and monitoring reasoning can be undermined by optimisation pressure~\citep{baker2025monitoring}. Our reasoning-effort and pre-thinking probe results bear on when a decision is fixed before or during reasoning. Linear probes are our read-out tool~\citep{alain2016probes}.

\paragraph{Distillation and trait transfer.} Knowledge distillation transfers behaviour from teacher to student~\citep{hinton2015distilling}, and LoRA makes such fine-tuning cheap~\citep{hu2021lora}. \citet{cloud2025subliminal} report that behavioural traits can pass through data that appears unrelated to them. Our Corpus~B experiment asks a related question: does a teacher's shortcut tendency pass through transcripts in which the teacher only ever takes the safe route?

\paragraph{Methodological commitments.} All scenarios and harm descriptions were authored by an uncensored model on remote hardware and are not reproduced here. Every result number in the text, tables and figures is generated programmatically from verified summary records and bound to them by a formula that an automated checker re-evaluates. We use exact paired McNemar tests~\citep{mcnemar1947}, Wilson intervals~\citep{wilson1927}, Newcombe intervals for paired differences~\citep{newcombe1998}, Holm correction~\citep{holm1979}, cluster-level sign-flip tests and Firth-penalised logistic models~\citep{firth1993}. The total compute cost was \$\fact{budget.billed} of a \$\fact{budget.cap} budget. Section~\ref{sec:limits} collects every threat to validity we know of.
