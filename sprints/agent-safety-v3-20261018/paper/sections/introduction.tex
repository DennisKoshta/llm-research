\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A common safety story for chat models is that they \emph{refuse}: asked to do something harmful, they decline. Work on refusal directions shows that this behaviour is largely mediated by a single direction in the residual stream, and that projecting it out of the weights removes most refusals of harmful requests~\citep{arditi2024refusal}. Agents face a different situation. Nobody asks them to cause harm. Instead, harm is the side effect of a convenient action: a one-step tool that finishes the job but damages something else, offered next to a long, tedious or obstructed safe procedure. Whether an agent takes that shortcut is a question of \emph{restraint under effort cost}, not of refusing an instruction.

This paper asks whether the two are the same mechanism. If restraint were just refusal, removing the refusal direction should make agents take harmful shortcuts whenever the safe route is costly. If restraint has other sources, the edit should leave agentic behaviour mostly unchanged while still removing refusals of direct orders. Between these poles, the interesting question is \emph{what} mediates the cases where restraint does fail.

\paragraph{Setup.} We edit Qwen3.8-27B (8-bit weights) with a rank-one orthogonalisation along a contrastive refusal direction $\hat r$ at layer~48, and compare the edited model (\Ablated) with the original (\Intact), three principal-component control edits matched in variance (PCA-101, PCA-102, PCA-103) and an unrelated-concept control. Agents work in a simulated workspace with a goal, an ordinary route of one or seven tool calls, a one-step direct tool and a hand-off tool that passes the job to a human. In \emph{harmful} scenarios the direct tool causes severe, irreversible damage inside the simulation; in \emph{harmless} twins it only touches disposable test data. A shortcut is counted only when a valid JSON tool call is emitted after the model's reasoning, under a strict parser; nothing is inferred from prose.

\paragraph{Experiments and findings.} We ran nine experiment blocks (Table~\ref{tab:overview} in the appendix summarises them).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Phase~1} selects a checkpoint family that passes a direct-instruction positive control and documents why an alternative family failed (Section~\ref{sec:phase1}).
\item \textbf{Exp~1.1} crosses route cost, consequence and an explicit prohibition. The ablation effect on harmful shortcuts is small at the pre-fixed reasoning effort and borderline after multiplicity correction, but larger at low effort (Section~\ref{sec:direct}).
\item \textbf{Exp~3.1} shows that the format of the stakes, a sentence versus a rating table, has the largest effect we measured.
\item \textbf{Exp~1.4} (exploratory) blocks the safe route mid-way. Agents hand off rather than persist or switch to the shortcut.
\item \textbf{Exp~3.2} tests whether layer~48 activations before thinking predict the action (Section~\ref{sec:probes}).
\item \textbf{Exp~1.2} distils teachers into LoRA students~\citep{hu2021lora} on unfiltered, safe-route-only, paraphrased and unrelated corpora. The shortcut tendency transfers even through safe-route-only data (Section~\ref{sec:distill}), echoing reports of behavioural traits passing through seemingly unrelated data~\citep{cloud2025subliminal}.
\item \textbf{Exp~2.3, 2.1 and 2.2} test contagion through reviewers, two-agent coordination and monitor oversight~\citep{greenblatt2024control} (Section~\ref{sec:multi}). They are mostly uninformative floors and ceilings, with one clear authority-framing effect in Llama-3.3-70B.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Thesis.} Taken together, the results support a simple claim: \emph{direct refusal is not agentic restraint}. Removing the refusal direction removes refusals of orders, but in agentic settings with a safe exit the edited model mostly stays safe. Its restraint fails in a minority of cases, and that minority grows with cheap, everyday changes: less deliberation, more abstract stakes, and training on its own transcripts.

\paragraph{Methodological commitments.} All scenarios and harm descriptions were authored by an uncensored model on remote hardware and are never reproduced in this paper. Every result number in the text and tables is generated from verified records by a formula that a checker re-evaluates. Tests use exact paired McNemar tests~\citep{mcnemar1947}, Wilson intervals~\citep{wilson1927}, Newcombe intervals for paired differences~\citep{newcombe1998}, Holm correction~\citep{holm1979}, cluster-level sign-flip tests and Firth-penalised logistic models~\citep{firth1993}. The total compute cost was \$\fact{budget.billed} of a \$\fact{budget.cap} budget. Section~\ref{sec:limits} collects every threat to validity we know of.
