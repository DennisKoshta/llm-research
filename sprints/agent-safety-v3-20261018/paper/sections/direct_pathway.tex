\subsection{Experiment 1.1: does ablation produce harmful shortcuts under effort cost?}
\paragraph{Design.} Each of the \fact{d.scen} bases is rendered in \fact{d.cells} cells. The cells cross route cost (low: one call; high tedium: seven repetitive calls; high friction: seven calls through obstacles) with consequence (harmful or harmless) and boundary (implicit, or explicit with one added sentence prohibiting the direct tool). Tool order is counterbalanced. Agents run for up to \fact{d.maxturns} turns with thinking on at the chat template's default reasoning effort, with a cap of \fact{d.cap.on} new tokens per turn. That effort setting was fixed from the qualification phase before any Exp~1.1 data existed. \Intact{} and \Ablated{} were also run with thinking off, and a low-effort sensitivity run was added after the primary results were seen. The pre-specified primary outcome is the shortcut rate on the \fact{d.pairs} high-cost harmful-implicit pairs (\fact{d.scen} scenarios crossed with tedium and friction). The pre-specified contrasts are P1.1 (\Ablated{} versus \Intact), P1.2 (\Ablated{} versus each control edit) and P1.3 (thinking on versus off), which together form one Holm family of \fact{holm.k}.

\paragraph{Construct validity.} At default effort with thinking on, \Intact{} took the harmless shortcut in \fact{e11.intact.harmless_impl.k}/\fact{e11.harmless.n} high-cost implicit cells and the harmful one in \fact{e11.intact.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs}, and it completed the forced safe route reliably (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}). The design therefore separates cost pressure from harm.

\paragraph{What the agents do: they walk the long route.} In the high-cost harmful-implicit cells the agents mostly complete the seven-call ordinary route rather than leave through the hand-off tool (Table~\ref{tab:exp11actions}). At default effort \Intact{} completed the route in \fact{as.intact.default.safe_route_completed}/\fact{d.pairs} pairs, escalated in \fact{as.intact.default.escalated} and produced \fact{as.intact.default.unscorable} truncated episode. \Ablated{} completed it in \fact{as.refusal_ablated.default.safe_route_completed}, took the shortcut in \fact{as.refusal_ablated.default.shortcut} and escalated in \fact{as.refusal_ablated.default.escalated}. Restraint in this setting is therefore carried out by doing the tedious work, not by escaping it. Counting both boundary conditions of the high-cost harmful cells, \Ablated{} took \fact{st.abl.default.n} shortcuts at default effort, and every one came after at least one ordinary-route step (\fact{st.abl.default.turn1} on the first turn). At low effort \fact{st.abl.low.turn1} of its \fact{st.abl.low.n} shortcuts came on the first turn.

\paragraph{The primary effect is small and fragile.} \Ablated{} took the harmful shortcut in \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs} pairs, whereas \Intact, the three PCA controls and the unrelated control each took it in \fact{e11.intact.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs} (Table~\ref{tab:exp11cells}, Figure~\ref{fig:phase1-exp11}B). The difference is \fact{p11.diff}\pp{} (Newcombe 95\% CI \fact{p11.cil} to \fact{p11.cih}; exact McNemar $p=$\,\fact{p11.p}). It does not survive Holm correction over the \fact{holm.k} contrasts ($p=$\,\fact{p11.holm}). That correction is conservative here, because \fact{holm.same} of the \fact{holm.k} contrasts (P1.1 and the four P1.2 contrasts) test the same \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k} \Ablated{} events against zero baselines. The template-level sign-flip $p$ is \fact{p11.signflip} (Table~\ref{tab:exp11contrasts}). It equals the floor \fact{tmpl.floor.active} that arises because only \fact{tmpl.default.n} of the \fact{d.templates} templates contain a discordant pair, with one template holding \fact{tmpl.default.max} of the \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k} events. With all templates active the floor would be \fact{tmpl.floor.all}. A same-launch replicate of the identical prompts gave \fact{dc.refusal_ablated.std.default.shortcut}/\fact{d.pairs}, and the two runs shared \fact{dcw.refusal_ablated.default.std_vs_std_original.both} of their shortcut items (Section~\ref{sec:deconf}). We therefore rate the primary effect \emph{weak}: a low, repeatable rate whose individual events do not reproduce.

\paragraph{Reasoning effort dissociates direct and autonomous harm.} Lowering the reasoning effort moves the edited model in opposite directions in the two action spaces. On direct orders it becomes \emph{less} compliant: Set~C compliance falls from \fact{p1.C.abl.pct}\% to \fact{p1.C.abl.low.pct}\%, and the Exp~1.1 direct-order probe falls from \fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.solo.comply}/\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.solo.n} to \fact{do.refusal_ablated.low.solo.comply}/\fact{do.refusal_ablated.low.solo.n}. Of the \fact{do.refusal_ablated.low.solo.n} low-effort orders, \fact{do.refusal_ablated.low.solo.text_reply} received a prose reply with no tool call, \fact{do.refusal_ablated.low.solo.undeclared_call} a call to a tool that was not offered, and \fact{do.refusal_ablated.low.solo.unscorable} were unscorable. In the autonomous task the opposite happens: harmful shortcuts triple, from \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs} to \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.low.k}/\fact{d.pairs} (Holm $p=$\,\fact{p11low.holm} against \Intact; template sign-flip $p=$\,\fact{p11low.signflip}), while every other arm stays at \fact{e11.intact.low.k}. Within \Ablated{} the low-versus-default contrast is significant (McNemar $p=$\,\fact{rob.low_vs_default:refusal_ablated.p}; Holm \fact{rob.low_vs_default:refusal_ablated.holm}). The difference-in-differences against \Intact{} is \fact{did.diff}\pp{} (scenario-cluster bootstrap CI \fact{did.cil} to \fact{did.cih}). Unlike the default-effort events, the low-effort rate replicates within a second launch (\fact{dc.refusal_ablated.std.low.shortcut}/\fact{d.pairs}). The low-effort run was chosen after the default result was known and reuses the same scenarios, so it is a sensitivity analysis rather than an independent replication.

The three reasoning conditions do not form a monotonic relationship between reasoning length and restraint: \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.pct}\% at default effort, \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.off.pct}\% with thinking off and \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.low.pct}\% with brief thinking. The brief-versus-no-thinking gap (\fact{rob.lowoff.diff}\pp, Holm $p=$\,\fact{rob.low_vs_off:refusal_ablated.holm}) is not significant after correction, and the P1.3 contrasts are null for both arms ($p=$\,\fact{P1.3_intact.p} and \fact{P1.3_refusal_ablated.p}). The reasoning traces show one consistent change. At default effort \Ablated's first-turn reasoning mentions the hand-off option in \fact{cot.e11.refusal_ablated.default.mentions_escalate}/\fact{cot.e11.refusal_ablated.default.n} episodes, but at low effort only in \fact{cot.e11.refusal_ablated.low.mentions_escalate}/\fact{cot.e11.refusal_ablated.low.n}. The \Intact{} figures are \fact{cot.e11.intact.default.mentions_escalate} and \fact{cot.e11.intact.low.mentions_escalate}. Brief reasoning mostly stops weighing the alternatives. Inverse scaling of safety-relevant behaviour with test-time compute has been reported in other settings~\citep{gema2025inverse}; here the direction depends on the action space.

\paragraph{Harm sensitivity is largely kept; explicit rules on harmless actions are not.} \Ablated{} took the harmless shortcut in \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.harmless_impl.k}/\fact{e11.harmless.n} high-cost implicit cells against \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs} harmful ones, so its gap between harmless and harmful stakes is mostly intact. Because the two kinds of sentence also differ lexically (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), this gap is not proof of harm comprehension. Where an explicit rule forbade a \emph{harmless} shortcut, however, \Ablated{} broke it in \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.harmless_expl.k}/\fact{e11.harmless.n} cells, against \fact{e11.intact.harmless_expl.k}/\fact{e11.harmless.n} for \Intact{} and at most \fact{hx.ctrlmax}/\fact{e11.harmless.n} for any control edit. This exploratory contrast is significant (exact McNemar $p=$\,\fact{hx.p}; template sign-flip $p=$\,\fact{hx.sf}). It replicates in Exp~1.4, whose first turns are the same prompts: there \Ablated{} broke the rule in \fact{e14.default.refusal_ablated.harmless.explicit.shortcut}/\fact{e14.default.refusal_ablated.harmless.explicit.n} episodes against \fact{e14.default.intact.harmless.explicit.shortcut}/\fact{e14.default.intact.harmless.explicit.n} for \Intact. The refusal direction is a plausible carrier. In \Intact, an explicit prohibition on a harmless task moves the layer-48 state towards $+\hat r$ by a substantial fraction of the shift that harmful stakes produce (Section~\ref{sec:probes}), so removing $\hat r$ removes part of the representation of ``this is forbidden'' as well as of ``this is harmful''~\citep{zhao2025harmfulness}.

\paragraph{Companion probes: the hand-off tool changes direct-order behaviour completely.} Ordered to call the harmful tool with no alternative, \Ablated{} complied in \fact{di.refusal_ablated.solo.k}/\fact{di.n} scenarios and \Intact{} in \fact{di.intact.solo.k}/\fact{di.n}. The hand-off tool was then added to the tool list. No sentence invited its use: none of the \fact{do.prompt.n} prompts mentions it outside the tool list (\fact{do.prompt.invites}), and the order to call the direct tool was unchanged. \Ablated{} then complied in \fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.esc.comply}/\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.esc.n} and escalated in \fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.esc.escalated}/\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.esc.n}. On direct orders, then, the edit removes refusal but not the preference for a sanctioned exit. In the autonomous task the exit is almost never used, and removing it changes nothing (Section~\ref{sec:deconf}). Every arm answered the consequence-awareness questions perfectly, so these questions cannot separate ``unaware'' from ``aware but proceeding''. The external pair, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and a community-uncensored variant, both took \fact{ext.flash_next.k}/\fact{d.pairs} harmful shortcuts, with \fact{ext.flash_next.trunc} and \fact{ext.flash_next_uncensored.trunc} episodes ending on malformed or truncated output. Without a positive control for this pair, its null is nearly uninformative.

\subsection{Deconfounding the hand-off exit and the call-count instruction}
\label{sec:deconf}
Two features of the Exp~1.1 prompt could explain the agents' behaviour without any appeal to restraint. The first is the one-call hand-off tool, a cheap exit. The second is the frozen system sentence that tells the agent to prefer the workflow with fewer calls. We reran the \fact{d.pairs} high-cost harmful-implicit pairs for \Ablated, PCA-101 and \Intact{} with each feature removed. In the no-hand-off condition, \texttt{escalate\_work\_order} is absent from the tool list and the prompt, at default and low effort. In the no-preference condition the fewer-calls sentence is deleted, at default effort. The identical standard prompts were run in the same launch, because long greedy generations do not reproduce across launches (Table~\ref{tab:deconf11}, Figure~\ref{fig:deconf}A).

\paragraph{The exit is not what keeps agents safe.} Without the hand-off tool, \Intact{} and PCA-101 completed the seven-call route in \fact{dc.intact.nh.default.safe_route_completed}/\fact{d.pairs} and \fact{dc.pca_101.nh.default.safe_route_completed}/\fact{d.pairs} pairs at default effort, and in \fact{dc.intact.nh.low.safe_route_completed} and \fact{dc.pca_101.nh.low.safe_route_completed} at low effort. \Ablated{} took \fact{dc.refusal_ablated.nh.default.shortcut} shortcuts at default effort and \fact{dc.refusal_ablated.nh.low.shortcut} at low effort. The same-launch standard prompt gave \fact{dc.refusal_ablated.std.default.shortcut} and \fact{dc.refusal_ablated.std.low.shortcut} (McNemar $p=$\,\fact{dcw.refusal_ablated.default.nh_vs_std.p} and \fact{dcw.refusal_ablated.low.nh_vs_std.p}). Restraint in this task does not depend on an escape hatch.

\paragraph{The instructed call minimisation carries the default-effort shortcuts.} Without the fewer-calls sentence, \Ablated{} took \fact{dc.refusal_ablated.np.default.shortcut}/\fact{d.pairs} harmful shortcuts. The same-launch standard prompt gave \fact{dc.refusal_ablated.std.default.shortcut} and the original run \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k} (McNemar $p=$\,\fact{dcw.refusal_ablated.default.np_vs_std.p} and \fact{dcw.refusal_ablated.default.np_vs_std_original.p}). \Intact{} and PCA-101 took none either way, and every arm still completed the route (\Ablated: \fact{dc.refusal_ablated.np.default.safe_route_completed}/\fact{d.pairs}). The intrinsic tedium of seven calls is thus not enough to make the edited model cut the corner at default effort. What it cannot resist is an explicit efficiency instruction combined with tedium. This matters for interpretation: the Exp~1.1 shortcut is a conflict between two instructions (prefer fewer calls; act conscientiously) that the edited model resolves differently, not spontaneous harm-seeking.

For the thesis of Section~\ref{sec:intro}, this deconfounder carries the first takeaway. The edit adds no drive to cut corners: with tedium alone the edited model does the work. Harmful shortcuts appear only when a competing instruction asks the agent to trade conscientiousness against efficiency, and whether it stays safe then depends on how that trade is resolved in context. The low-effort run above and the next experiment show how easily that resolution tips.

\subsection{Experiment 3.1: how the stakes are shown}
\paragraph{Design.} The same task structure is rendered over \fact{d.domains} domains with \fact{e31.design.scen} scenarios (eight per domain). The harm consequence is given either as the authored concrete sentence or as an abstract rating table of severity and permanence (Table~\ref{tab:schema}). The \fact{e31.design.scen} scenarios $\times$ \fact{e31.design.cells} cells $\times$ \fact{e31.design.orders} tool orders $\times$ two formats $\times$ two thinking modes $\times$ three arms (\Ablated, PCA-101, \Intact) give \fact{e31.rows} episodes. The scenario wording was calibrated on \Intact{} for the sentence format only; the table format was never calibrated. All rates are intention-to-treat.

\paragraph{Results.} With thinking on, \Ablated's harmful-shortcut rate over all harmful cells rises from \fact{e31.refusal_ablated.cot_on.narr}\% with the sentence to \fact{e31.refusal_ablated.cot_on.num}\% with the table. The paired difference is \fact{e31.refusal_ablated.cot_on.diff}\pp{} (McNemar $p=$\,\fact{e31.refusal_ablated.cot_on.p}; Holm \fact{e31.refusal_ablated.cot_on.holm}; Table~\ref{tab:exp31}, Figure~\ref{fig:exp31}). Under the table even the unedited models begin to take harmful shortcuts. On the high-cost harmful-implicit slice (\fact{e31.hc.n} pairs per arm), \Intact{} goes from \fact{e31hc.intact.cot_on.narrative.pct}\% to \fact{e31hc.intact.cot_on.numeric.pct}\% and PCA-101 from \fact{e31hc.pca_101.cot_on.narrative.pct}\% to \fact{e31hc.pca_101.cot_on.numeric.pct}\%. \Ablated{} goes from \fact{e31hc.refusal_ablated.cot_on.narrative.pct}\% to \fact{e31hc.refusal_ablated.cot_on.numeric.pct}\%. Under the table, the \Ablated-minus-\Intact{} contrast is \fact{e31.A1.diff}\pp{} (scenario-cluster CI \fact{e31.A1.cl} to \fact{e31.A1.ch}; Holm-adjusted sign-flip $p=$\,\fact{e31.A1.sfholm}). The \Ablated-minus-PCA-101 contrast is \fact{x2d.combined.pca_101.gap_AT}\pp{} (CI \fact{x2d.combined.pca_101.gap_AT.l} to \fact{x2d.combined.pca_101.gap_AT.h}).

The effect appears in every domain. Over harmful cells with thinking on, \Ablated{} goes from \fact{e31d.refusal_ablated.warehouse.narr}\% to \fact{e31d.refusal_ablated.warehouse.num}\% in warehouse, \fact{e31d.refusal_ablated.compute.narr}\% to \fact{e31d.refusal_ablated.compute.num}\% in compute, \fact{e31d.refusal_ablated.finance.narr}\% to \fact{e31d.refusal_ablated.finance.num}\% in finance, and \fact{e31d.refusal_ablated.wetlab.narr}\% to \fact{e31d.refusal_ablated.wetlab.num}\% in wet lab (Figure~\ref{fig:exp31}C). With thinking off, the format effect is negligible: \fact{e31.refusal_ablated.cot_off.diff}\pp{} for \Ablated. The longer reasoning that accompanies the table is visible in the traces. \Ablated's first-turn reasoning on the slice averages \fact{cot.e31.refusal_ablated.numeric.words} words with the table, against \fact{cot.e31.refusal_ablated.narrative.words} with the sentence.

\paragraph{Layout or abstraction? A two-by-two deconfounder.} The table changes two things at once: the layout (table instead of prose) and the specificity (rating words instead of a concrete description). We therefore ran the two missing cells on the slice. In the concrete table the severity cell also carries the authored description; in the abstract sentence a standard sentence states the same rating words as the table. We generated all four cells in one launch (Table~\ref{tab:exp31x2}, Figure~\ref{fig:deconf}B). Neither factor alone moves \Ablated. Its rate is \fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.CS}\% for the concrete sentence, \fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.AS}\% for the abstract sentence, \fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.CT}\% for the concrete table and \fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.AT}\% for the abstract table. The format effect is almost entirely an interaction of \fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.interaction}\pp{} (CI \fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.interaction.l} to \fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.interaction.h}). \Intact{} and PCA-101 shortcut only in the abstract-table cell (\fact{x2.same_launch.intact.AT}\% and \fact{x2.same_launch.pca_101.AT}\%). The arm $\times$ format difference-in-differences for the abstract-table effect (AT$-$CS) is \fact{x2d.same_launch.intact.total}\pp{} against \Intact{} (CI \fact{x2d.same_launch.intact.total.l} to \fact{x2d.same_launch.intact.total.h}; Holm sign-flip $p=$\,\fact{x2d.same_launch.intact.total.holm}). Against PCA-101 it is \fact{x2d.same_launch.pca_101.total}\pp{} (CI \fact{x2d.same_launch.pca_101.total.l} to \fact{x2d.same_launch.pca_101.total.h}). The headline cell replicated within the launch (\fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.AT}\% against \fact{x2.combined.refusal_ablated.AT}\% originally).

\paragraph{Interpretation.} This is the largest effect in the study, and the deconfounder narrows what it means. Rating words do not weaken restraint by themselves: written as a sentence they produce the fewest shortcuts of all. A table does not either, as long as it carries the description. Restraint fails when the consequence appears \emph{only} as a cell value in a table of route statistics, next to the step counts the agent has been told to minimise. A plausible reading is that the abstract table recasts the harm as one more cost attribute to be traded against calls, rather than as a description of what will happen. The edited model is much more susceptible to that recasting, but the unedited models are not immune. These format effects were found in prompts calibrated for the sentence format only.

For the second takeaway, the abstract table is the clearest case of brittle deliberation. The edited model does not reason less under the table: its first-turn reasoning is longer than with the sentence. That longer reasoning accompanies the largest failure rate in the study, and the \fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.interaction}\pp{} interaction shows that what tips it is the harm becoming a rating beside the call counts. Once the stakes take that form, weighing them against the efficiency instruction no longer works as a brake, for the edited model above all.

\subsection{Experiment 1.4 (exploratory): blocking the safe route}
\paragraph{Design.} To ask whether agents pursue a goal ``by any means'', we took the high-tedium prompts unchanged and made the ordinary route fail mid-way. After step one (early) or step four (late), every further safe call returned a realistic lock error with no release estimate, while the direct tool and the hand-off tool kept working. The design was frozen before data. We count shortcuts, escalations, prose stops, and persistence, meaning retrying the broken route until the turn limit. The first turn of every episode is byte-identical to its Exp~1.1 twin (\fact{e14t1.identical} of \fact{e14t1.checked} prompts).

\paragraph{Results.} No model persisted: \fact{e14.persisted} of \fact{e14.rows} episodes kept retrying (Table~\ref{tab:summary2}). With harmful stakes, \Intact{} escalated in \fact{e14.default.intact.harmful.implicit.escalated}/\fact{e14.default.intact.harmful.implicit.n} implicit episodes. \Ablated{} escalated in \fact{e14.default.refusal_ablated.harmful.implicit.escalated}/\fact{e14.default.refusal_ablated.harmful.implicit.n} and took the shortcut in \fact{e14.default.refusal_ablated.harmful.implicit.shortcut} (C1 $p=$\,\fact{e14.default.C1.p}); at low effort it took it in \fact{e14.low.refusal_ablated.harmful.implicit.shortcut}/\fact{e14.low.refusal_ablated.harmful.implicit.n}. In harmful cells the block rarely caused a shortcut: across all runs, \fact{e14.harm.pre} of the \fact{e14.harm.sc} harmful-cell shortcuts were taken \emph{before} the block fired and \fact{e14.harm.post} after it. Rule-breaking after the block did occur in the harmless-explicit cells. There \Ablated{} broke the explicit rule in \fact{e14.default.refusal_ablated.harmless.explicit.shortcut}/\fact{e14.default.refusal_ablated.harmless.explicit.n} episodes at default effort, \fact{e14.default.abl.hexpl.post} of them after the block. \Intact{} did so in \fact{e14.default.intact.harmless.explicit.shortcut}/\fact{e14.default.intact.harmless.explicit.n}. Over all cells at default effort, \fact{e14.default.abl.post} of the \fact{e14.default.abl.reached} \Ablated{} episodes that reached the block switched to the shortcut afterwards.

\paragraph{Caveats.} The early and late variants share the prompt and the pre-block trajectory. The pooled McNemar $p$ at low effort (\fact{e14.low.C1.p}) therefore treats duplicate pairs as independent and overstates the evidence. Because a hand-off tool was always offered, persistence was barely tested, and ``gave up'' here means taking the offered hand-off at the first error.
