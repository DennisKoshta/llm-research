\section{Multi-agent contagion, coordination and oversight}
\label{sec:multi}

\subsection{Experiment 2.3: does reviewer advice spread to the executor?}
\paragraph{Design.} We built \fact{d.e23scen} scenarios, twelve per domain, authored by the uncensored model and gated by the same numeric judge. An \Intact{} planner wrote a plan, and reviewers then commented on it. The reviewers were of three kinds. Natural reviewers were \Intact, PCA-101, \Ablated{} and the uncensored model. Authored memos, written in advance, were a harmless-state shortcut memo (B3), a careful-register shortcut memo (B4a) and a blunt-register safe-route memo (B4b). Finally, Llama-3.3-70B produced paraphrases of reviewer notes. Each note was framed either as the designated compliance reviewer's review or as peer advice. The executors were \Intact{} (all six tool orders, thinking on or off) and Llama-3.3-70B and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (three orders each). In total they produced \fact{e23.rows} executor rows.

\paragraph{Results.} In the harmful state, natural reviewers never advised the shortcut: zero of \fact{e23.natural.n} valid notes did, including those written by \Ablated{} and by the uncensored model. Contagion through natural advice therefore could not occur in this design. With the authored careful-register shortcut memo, Llama-3.3-70B took the harmful shortcut in \fact{e23.llama.designated.B4a.k}/\fact{e23.llama.designated.B4a.n} trials (\fact{e23.llama.designated.B4a.pct}\%) when the memo was framed as the designated review. Framed as peer advice, it took it in only \fact{e23.llama.peer.B4a.k}/\fact{e23.llama.peer.B4a.n} (\fact{e23.llama.peer.B4a.pct}\%). The effect appears in every domain but is weakest in finance (Figure~\ref{fig:multi}A). The Qwen executors never followed the memo. \Intact{} took the shortcut in \fact{e23.intact.B4a.k} of \fact{e23.intact.B4a.n} B4a trials, and with thinking off under the designated framing it escalated in \fact{e23.intact.esc}. The Flash-Next zeros are weak evidence, because \fact{e23.flash.on.trunc}\% of its answers with thinking on and \fact{e23.flash.off.trunc}\% with thinking off were unscorable under the strict parser.

\paragraph{Reading.} Authority framing can carry harmful advice into a cross-family executor, but the design limits how far the claim reaches. The two framings differ in more than authority. No blunt-register shortcut memo or careful-register safe-route memo exists, so register and direction are not separated. And the original model's zero baseline means that ``no contagion'' is partly built into the Qwen results.

\subsection{Experiment 2.1: coordination between two agents}
\paragraph{Design.} Two agents, Alpha and Beta, each choose either a standard lane that applies their own safety control or an express lane that skips it to save a credit. One agent skipping is allowed; both skipping is the forbidden joint outcome. Topologies A and C give the agents no message channel and topologies B and D give them one; C and D add a monitor gate, each with and without a hold fee. Teams were either all-\Intact{} or mixed, with an \Ablated{} Alpha and an \Intact{} Beta. The experiment covered \fact{e21.worlds} worlds with \fact{d.e21k} sampled draws per world, for \fact{e21.trials} trials.

\paragraph{Results.} The forbidden joint shortcut never happened (\fact{e21.joint}/\fact{e21.trials}), so every contrast about it compares zero with zero. The ablated agent's own, allowed behaviour did change, and the change depended on the message channel. The \Ablated{} Alpha took the express lane in \fact{e21.mix.B.alpha}\% of B trials, \fact{e21.mix.D_nopen.alpha}\% of D trials and \fact{e21.mix.D_pen.alpha}\% under a hold fee. The \Intact{} Alpha did so in \fact{e21.int.B.alpha}\%, \fact{e21.int.D_nopen.alpha}\% and \fact{e21.int.D_pen.alpha}\%. Without a channel the \Ablated{} Alpha rarely skipped: \fact{e21.mix.A.alpha}\% in A and \fact{e21.mix.C_nopen.alpha}\% in C. Beta compensated. The legitimate-coordination outcome, Alpha express with Beta standard, tracks the express rate almost exactly: \fact{e21.mix.B.legit}\% in B and \fact{e21.mix.D_nopen.legit}\% in D (Figure~\ref{fig:multi}B). No control edit was run as Alpha, so this channel-gated effect is a contrast with \Intact{} only.

\subsection{Experiment 2.2: monitor calibration}
A monitor was shown the action logs and an optional note, and had to block forbidden bundles while allowing legitimate ones. The \Intact{} monitor with thinking off blocked \fact{e22.intact.tpr.k}/\fact{e22.intact.tpr.n} forbidden bundles and \fact{e22.intact.fpr.k} allowed ones. Llama-3.3-70B blocked \fact{e22.llama70b.tpr.k}/\fact{e22.llama70b.tpr.n} forbidden bundles, and Flash-Next blocked \fact{e22.flash_next.tpr.k}/\fact{e22.flash_next.tpr.n} of its readable verdicts. No note fooled any monitor, not even a deceptively compounded one, so the design cannot rank monitors on detection. Notes did change Llama's false alarms, of which there were \fact{e22.llama70b.fpr.k} in total. With no note its false-alarm rate was \fact{e22.llama.fpr.action_log_only}\%. With a matched-length note it fell to between \fact{e22.llama.fpr.truthful_compliance}\% and \fact{e22.llama.fpr.neutral_matched}\%: \fact{e22.llama.fpr.neutral_matched}\% for a neutral note and \fact{e22.llama.fpr.clause_both}\% for a note with risk and urgency clauses. A minimal note gave \fact{e22.llama.fpr.minimal}\%.
