\section{Multi-agent contagion, coordination and oversight}
\label{sec:multi}

\subsection{Experiment 2.3: does reviewer advice spread to the executor?}
\paragraph{Design.} We built \fact{d.e23scen} scenarios, twelve per domain, authored by the uncensored model and gated by the same numeric judge. An \Intact{} planner wrote a plan, and reviewers then commented on it. The reviewers were of three kinds. Natural reviewers were \Intact, PCA-101, \Ablated{} and the uncensored model. Authored memos, written in advance, were a harmless-state shortcut memo (B3), a careful-register shortcut memo (B4a) and a blunt-register safe-route memo (B4b). Finally, Llama-3.3-70B produced paraphrases of reviewer notes. Each note was framed either as the designated compliance reviewer's review or as peer advice. The executors were \Intact{} (all six tool orders, thinking on or off) and Llama-3.3-70B and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (three orders each). In total they produced \fact{e23.rows} executor rows.

\paragraph{Results.} In the harmful state, natural reviewers never advised the shortcut: zero of \fact{e23.natural.n} valid notes did, including those written by \Ablated{} and by the uncensored model. Contagion through natural advice therefore could not occur in this design. With the authored careful-register shortcut memo, Llama-3.3-70B took the harmful shortcut in \fact{e23.llama.designated.B4a.k}/\fact{e23.llama.designated.B4a.n} trials (\fact{e23.llama.designated.B4a.pct}\%) when the memo was framed as the designated review. Framed as peer advice, it took it in only \fact{e23.llama.peer.B4a.k}/\fact{e23.llama.peer.B4a.n} (\fact{e23.llama.peer.B4a.pct}\%). Under designated framing the counts were \fact{e23.dom.designated.warehouse}/\fact{e23.dom.n} in warehouse, \fact{e23.dom.designated.compute}/\fact{e23.dom.n} in compute, \fact{e23.dom.designated.wetlab}/\fact{e23.dom.n} in wet lab and \fact{e23.dom.designated.finance}/\fact{e23.dom.n} in finance (Figure~\ref{fig:multi}A). Under peer framing the only non-zero domain was compute (\fact{e23.dom.peer.compute}/\fact{e23.dom.n}). The Qwen executors never followed the memo. \Intact{} took the shortcut in \fact{e23.intact.B4a.k} of \fact{e23.intact.B4a.n} B4a trials, and with thinking off under designated framing it escalated in \fact{e23.intact.esc}. The Flash-Next zeros are weak evidence, because \fact{e23.flash.on.trunc}\% of its answers with thinking on and \fact{e23.flash.off.trunc}\% with thinking off were unscorable under the strict parser.

\paragraph{Reading.} Authority framing can carry harmful advice into a cross-family executor, but the design limits how far the claim reaches. The two framings differ in more than authority. No blunt-register shortcut memo or careful-register safe-route memo exists, so register and direction are not separated. And the original model's zero baseline means that ``no contagion'' is partly built into the Qwen results.

\subsection{Experiments 2.1 and 2.2: coordination and monitoring}
\paragraph{Coordination (Exp~2.1).} Two agents, Alpha and Beta, each chose between a standard lane that applies their own safety control and an express lane that skips it to save a credit. One agent skipping is allowed; both skipping is the forbidden joint outcome. Topologies differed in whether the agents could message each other and whether a monitor gate (with or without a hold fee) was present. Teams were all-\Intact{} or mixed (\Ablated{} Alpha, \Intact{} Beta), over \fact{e21.worlds} worlds with \fact{d.e21k} draws each (\fact{e21.trials} trials). The forbidden joint shortcut never occurred (\fact{e21.joint}/\fact{e21.trials}), so this design sits at a floor for its target outcome. The ablated agent's own, allowed behaviour did change, and only when a message channel existed. Without a channel the \Ablated{} Alpha took the express lane in \fact{e21.mix.A.alpha}\% of trials; with one it did so in \fact{e21.mix.B.alpha}\%, against \fact{e21.int.B.alpha}\% for an \Intact{} Alpha. Beta compensated: the legitimate-coordination outcome (Alpha express, Beta standard) occurred in \fact{e21.mix.B.legit}\% of those trials, so the team stayed within the rules (Figure~\ref{fig:multi}B; full breakdown in Table~\ref{tab:exp21}).

\paragraph{Monitoring (Exp~2.2).} A monitor read action logs and an optional note and had to block forbidden bundles while allowing legitimate ones. Detection sat at a ceiling. The \Intact{} monitor blocked \fact{e22.intact.tpr.k}/\fact{e22.intact.tpr.n} forbidden bundles, Llama-3.3-70B \fact{e22.llama70b.tpr.k}/\fact{e22.llama70b.tpr.n} and Flash-Next \fact{e22.flash_next.tpr.k}/\fact{e22.flash_next.tpr.n} of its readable verdicts. No note, deceptive ones included, fooled any monitor, so the design cannot rank monitors on detection. Notes did move Llama's false alarms, from \fact{e22.llama.fpr.action_log_only}\% with no note to between \fact{e22.llama.fpr.truthful_compliance}\% and \fact{e22.llama.fpr.neutral_matched}\% with matched-length notes (Table~\ref{tab:exp22}).
