\begin{abstract}
Removing a language model's refusal direction reliably removes its refusals of direct requests. Does it also remove the restraint an \emph{agent} shows when a harmful one-step shortcut competes with a long but safe route? We study this question in Qwen3.8-27B with a rank-one weight edit along the layer~48 refusal direction, \fact{d.arms} matched checkpoints (the original, the edited model, three variance-matched principal-component control edits and an unrelated-concept control), \fact{d.scen} authored multi-turn tool-use scenarios with severe simulated harm, and nine pre-registered or exploratory experiment blocks. Every reported number is recomputed from raw transcripts and bound to its record by formula.

The edit passes a clean direct-instruction positive control (\fact{p1.C.abl.k}/\fact{p1.C.abl.n} restricted-tool compliance versus \fact{p1.C.int.k}/\fact{p1.C.int.n} for the original). In agentic scenarios with a hand-off option, however, the effect is small: at the pre-fixed reasoning effort the edited model takes the harmful shortcut in \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs} high-cost pairs versus \fact{e11.intact.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs} for every other checkpoint (Holm-adjusted $p=$\,\fact{p11.holm}). Three factors mediate whether restraint fails. \emph{Reasoning effort}: a post-hoc low-effort run raises the rate to \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.low.k}/\fact{d.pairs}. \emph{Stakes abstraction}: replacing the harm sentence by a rating table raises the edited model's harmful-shortcut rate from \fact{e31.refusal_ablated.cot_on.narr}\% to \fact{e31.refusal_ablated.cot_on.num}\% with thinking on. \emph{Distillation}: students fine-tuned on the edited teacher's safe-route-only transcripts take the shortcut in \fact{e12.B.refusal_ablated.r32.pct}\% ($r{=}32$) and \fact{e12.B.refusal_ablated.r8.pct}\% ($r{=}8$) of held-out trials, against \fact{e12.B.intact.r32.pct}\% for students of the original teacher. When the safe route is blocked, no model persists and none switches to the shortcut after the block. Pre-thinking activations add nothing beyond the scenario cell when thinking is on (AUROC \fact{e32A.cot_on.probe} versus \fact{e32A.cot_on.design}). The multi-agent experiments sit at a floor or ceiling (\fact{e21.joint}/\fact{e21.trials} forbidden joint shortcuts; a monitor catching \fact{e22.intact.tpr.k}/\fact{e22.intact.tpr.n}). The one clear contagion is Llama-3.3-70B following an authored memo framed as a designated review (\fact{e23.llama.designated.B4a.pct}\% versus \fact{e23.llama.peer.B4a.pct}\% as peer advice). We report all null, floor and borderline results alongside the positive ones and list the threats to validity in a dedicated section.
\end{abstract}
