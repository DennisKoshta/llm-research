\begin{abstract}
Removing a language model's refusal direction from its weights, a technique known in the open-weights community as \emph{abliteration}~\citep{labonne2024abliteration}, reliably removes its refusals of direct requests. A common assumption is that it also turns an agent into an uninhibited corner-cutter. We test this in Qwen3.8-27B with an abliteration-style rank-one weight edit along the layer~48 refusal direction, in tool-use tasks where a harmful one-step shortcut competes with a long but safe route. The design uses \fact{d.arms} matched checkpoints (the original, the edited model, three variance-matched principal-component control edits and an unrelated-concept control), \fact{d.scen} authored multi-turn tool-use scenarios with severe simulated harm, nine experiment blocks and two deconfounder slices. Every reported number is recomputed from raw transcripts and bound to its record by formula. The results support a three-part account.

\emph{First, the edit removes a prohibition brake, not harm evaluation or the willingness to do tedious work.} The edited model obeys direct harmful orders with no alternative (\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.solo.comply}/\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.solo.n}) and breaks explicit rules on harmless actions far more often than the original (\fact{e11.refusal_ablated.harmless_expl.k}/\fact{e11.harmless.n} against \fact{e11.intact.harmless_expl.k}/\fact{e11.harmless.n}); in the original model, an explicit prohibition shifts the layer-48 state towards the refusal direction. Yet the edited model still separates harmful from harmless shortcuts, escalates every direct order (\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.esc.escalated}/\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.esc.n}) once an uninvited hand-off tool is listed, and in autonomous tasks completes the seven-call safe route in \fact{as.refusal_ablated.default.safe_route_completed}/\fact{d.pairs} high-cost harmful pairs (\fact{as.intact.default.safe_route_completed}/\fact{d.pairs} for the original). Its few harmful shortcuts (\fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs}; \fact{e11.intact.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs} for every other checkpoint; Holm $p=$\,\fact{p11.holm}) do not reproduce item by item. They vanish (\fact{dc.refusal_ablated.np.default.shortcut}/\fact{d.pairs}) when the instruction to prefer fewer calls is deleted, and removing the hand-off tool changes nothing.

\emph{Second, without the brake, restraint rests on in-context deliberation, and that deliberation is brittle} under ordinary pressures that compete with conscientiousness. \emph{Brief reasoning}: lowering the effort reduces direct-order compliance (\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.solo.comply}/\fact{do.refusal_ablated.default.solo.n} to \fact{do.refusal_ablated.low.solo.comply}/\fact{do.refusal_ablated.low.solo.n}) while tripling autonomous harmful shortcuts (\fact{e11.refusal_ablated.default.k}/\fact{d.pairs} to \fact{e11.refusal_ablated.low.k}/\fact{d.pairs}). In every arm, brief reasoning stops weighing the hand-off option, but only the edited model then cuts the corner. \emph{Abstract stakes}: a rating table raises the edited model's harmful-shortcut rate on the high-cost slice from \fact{e31hc.refusal_ablated.cot_on.narrative.pct}\% to \fact{e31hc.refusal_ablated.cot_on.numeric.pct}\%, and also pushes the original model (\fact{e31hc.intact.cot_on.narrative.pct}\% to \fact{e31hc.intact.cot_on.numeric.pct}\%) and a control edit (\fact{e31hc.pca_101.cot_on.narrative.pct}\% to \fact{e31hc.pca_101.cot_on.numeric.pct}\%) into harm. A layout $\times$ specificity deconfounder shows an interaction: neither a table that keeps the concrete description (\fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.CT}\%) nor a sentence of rating words (\fact{x2.same_launch.refusal_ablated.AS}\%) produces the effect.

\emph{Third, a decision made during reasoning is hard to read before it and easy to pass on through it.} With thinking on, pre-thinking activations add no detectable information beyond the scenario cell. Students trained only on the edited teacher's safe-route transcripts take the held-out shortcut in \fact{e12.B.refusal_ablated.r32.pct}\% ($r{=}32$) and \fact{e12.B.refusal_ablated.r8.pct}\% ($r{=}8$) of trials, against \fact{e12.B.intact.r32.pct}\% for students of the original teacher, and paraphrasing the teacher's reasoning removes the transfer. The multi-agent experiments sit at a floor or ceiling; the one clear contagion is Llama-3.3-70B following an authored memo framed as a designated review (\fact{e23.llama.designated.B4a.pct}\% versus \fact{e23.llama.peer.B4a.pct}\% as peer advice). We report all null, floor and borderline results and list the threats to validity.
\end{abstract}
