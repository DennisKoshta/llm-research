\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Removing the refusal direction from Qwen3.8-27B removes refusals of direct orders almost completely, but it does not remove agentic restraint. With a safe exit available and the stakes written out as a sentence, the edited agent mostly kept to the long route or handed the job to a human, and the pre-registered effect on harmful shortcuts was small and borderline. Restraint failed more often under three conditions: when the model reasoned briefly rather than at its default depth, when the stakes were given as an abstract rating rather than a description, and in student models trained on the edited model's own transcripts, even when those transcripts showed only safe behaviour. None of these conditions is exotic.

For oversight, the results counsel modesty. A pre-thinking probe added nothing beyond knowing the task when the model reasons, and the monitors and agent teams in our tests were never challenged hard enough to fail. The clearest multi-agent risk was a large model deferring to authoritative-sounding advice. The most useful next steps are fourfold: new scenarios with both efforts fixed in advance and several samples per item; controls that separate description from vividness in the stakes format; controls that separate transcript length from teacher identity in distillation; and multi-agent designs that are able to fail.
