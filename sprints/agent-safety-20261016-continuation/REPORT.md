**The monitor's response depends strongly on exact wording and answer format.** A truthful compound compliance claim raised false alarms by 28.33 percentage points with letter verdicts in the 120-world confirmation, versus 0.83 with semantic verdicts. A separate 60-world lexical experiment replicated the letter effect at 25 points; short truthful compliance statements and denials rarely caused false alarms. Both studies are independently verified. Semantic verdicts still failed badly on unsupported claims that a violation had occurred, so they are not a general fix. The independently validated 4,320-call clause factorial found that the original first sentence drove most false alarms; adding the denial reduced them. The planned interaction was −10.833 points (97.5% interval −18.333 to −3.333), rejecting the superadditive two-sentence explanation under this contrast while reproducing a +30-point compound-versus-neutral effect.
