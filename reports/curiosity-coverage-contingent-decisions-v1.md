## What a native follow-up would still need

The existing [48-response qualification](epistemic-readout-qualification-v1-results.md) remains failed. More fundamentally, separately generated answers can reconstruct facts from the prompt. Their correctness cannot establish preserved knowledge or appraisal at the moment of choice.

A possible inexpensive instrument check would decode candidate compatibility from already saved, closed-study activations. This would test accessible information, not subjective probabilities, belief use, information-gain estimates or motivational dissociation. A separate definition must be fixed before fitting; this note selects neither a decoder nor its outcomes. Even successful decoding would not admit a native intervention or validate the readout under steering.

The existing `decision` capture is retrospective. The trace code stores the preceding forward after observing a nonwhitespace final token or EOS, and later closing-think delimiters can replace the retained snapshot. The hidden vector precedes that token, but identifying the retained boundary uses subsequent output. It is usable for explicitly retrospective measurement development, not as an unchanged prospective intervention trigger. A future first-closing-delimiter forward would be a new, declared boundary requiring validation.

At the original prequestion record boundary, identical prefixes produce identical states regardless of the subsequent sampling seed. Variation after reasoning comes from different generated histories and can encode intended actions. Any native study must address that distinction using a prospectively fixed boundary, action-label counterbalancing and evidence-matched comparisons. Merely showing a state-to-action correlation is insufficient.

A causal native test would require an outcome-blind definition of native-range component changes, identity and same-state controls, matched nuisance perturbations, actual optional choices and contemporaneous epistemic measurements. Mandatory-use comparisons remain useful. Preserving a limited readout supports only preservation of that measured information; it is not proof of complete belief equivalence. No functional outcome here establishes subjective experience.
