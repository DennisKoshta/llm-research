## Stage 2: representation and causal experiments

Use the diagnosis to freeze a simpler, comprehensible measurement of known-versus-new information. Test held-out facts and prompt forms. Include recognition of the underlying facts, label/polarity counterbalancing, and controls for generic certainty, action instruction, response labels, and sentence length. Identify representations on development data independently of downstream information-choice effects. Separate extraction, layer/dose selection, and held-out evaluation.

First establish cross-template and cross-content decodability, then use controlled interventions (including sham, matched random directions and plausible competing concepts) and activation patching to test whether information judgments or optional choices depend on those representations. Where possible test both judgment polarities and two model sizes. Changes confined to one output label, wording, or format do not establish information-state regulation. Report damage to fact recognition and other competence controls.

A paper claim requires a concrete mechanistic result supported by held-out evidence, causal controls, robustness, and a clearly stated scope. Statistical significance or a large steering effect alone is insufficient. Any narrower claim or necessary redesign will be documented before new evaluation data are collected.
