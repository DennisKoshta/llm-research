# Task revision 2: identification fixed; accounting remains unvalidated

October 6, 2026

The final allowed task revision fixed identification, useful-clue selection, and service-menu comprehension in this development sample. Accounting accuracy remains below the frozen threshold, so **the overall advancement check failed and no new behavioral baseline was launched**. No curiosity/satisfaction intervention was tested.

## What changed

Machines now include exact lookup tables for the relevant inputs, removing the need to perform modular arithmetic. Services explicitly describe what they reveal about the hidden candidate, with identical descriptions in behavioral and diagnostic prompts. Separate questions check target-service and repeated-observation mappings. Accounting uses 200 distinct transaction contexts with individual purchases listed and four shuffled possible balances.

Model weights and revision, thinking-disabled inference, BF16 precision, response-format instructions, prices, hidden tasks, and sampling settings stayed fixed. The same development mysteries were reused. These results are not held-out confirmation, and lookup-supported machines should not be described as a test of unaided arithmetic or as strongly distinct from object-table identification.

For context, the previous revision scored 31% on machine identification, 49% on machine instrumental selection, and 19% on object instrumental selection. The combined presentation changes solved those observed development failures. This comparison does not isolate which individual wording change caused the improvement.

Accounting scored **145/200 overall (72.5%)**. The two family columns are disjoint allocations of accounting problems, not evidence that arithmetic depends on mystery type. These controls are broader than the original single repeated word problem and should not be interpreted as a directly comparable score increase from that earlier prompt.

An exploratory breakdown found 51/51 correct zero-price cases and 94/149 correct nonzero-price cases. This localizes the remaining practical problem to handling deductions in these controls, but does not establish its precise cognitive cause. The observed results do not justify willingness-to-pay claims or general conclusions about the model's mathematical ability.

The command-line advancement check was exercised and refused to create a behavioral manifest. ## Recommended next research decision

Declare the current priced assay unvalidated and preserve this result. For the next prospective protocol amendment, prioritize the already-planned one-credit information-choice assay: choosing the answer to one mystery makes the other answer unavailable. This creates a concrete opportunity cost without requiring multi-step point arithmetic. It still needs its own comprehension and preference calibration, fixed advancement criteria, and a bounded development allowance; it has not been implemented or run here.

Showing explicit before/after point balances is another possible instrument change, but it would require a prospective amendment and validation of tradeoff comprehension. Neither route should be described as the failed accounting checks having passed. Candidate extraction and psychological interventions remain deferred.
