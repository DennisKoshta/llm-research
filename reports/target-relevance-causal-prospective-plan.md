## Claim and boundaries

The candidate claim is that a specified residual-stream component causally contributes to appraisal and instructed use of information about a designated target, across object and machine tasks. The task holds accurate-report entropy, full-world information gain, initial target uncertainty, and stated reliability constant while target gain varies. Its four cells are `(u,q) = (0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1)`, with target gain `[0,1,1,0]`; `u` identifies the independent report and `q` the offered report.

This does not isolate subjective confidence, eliminate every relational-computation account, identify a unique value representation, or demonstrate intrinsic curiosity or inner experience. Instrument choices follow an explicit information goal. A large additive steering effect alone is insufficient for the stronger abstraction claim. The main intervention must replace one component with the corresponding component of a genuine counterfactual donor, followed by fresh reasoning.

### Calibration schedule and decision

At the fixed site, test only doses **0.5 and 1.0** times the frozen selected information mean-contrast norm, both signs. Original-axis scale comes from its original extraction; fallback scale comes from its calibration fit. Controls use the same signed scalar at each dose. Fix random unit-vector seeds 3, 5, and 7 before outcomes.

Instrument calibration has 26 arms: baseline and zero sham, plus information, `u`, `q`, and three random directions, each at both signs of both doses. The previous 16-arm sketch did not match controls to dose 0.5 and is superseded. Memory calibration has six arms: baseline, zero sham, and information at both signs of both doses. With 12 indices per family and four cells, these schedules total **3,072 continuations** before any duplicate-world aggregation.

For each source gain, evaluate the sign intended to reverse that source appraisal: positive information dose for gain zero, negative dose for gain one. A valid counterfactual response is report rather than stop for gain-zero instrument trials, and stop rather than report for gain-one trials. Invalid responses and rereading receive zero credit. Score controls with the same intended counterfactual label and signed scalar. The other sign is a reinforcement check and is still included in validity and competence checks.

A dose passes calibration only if:

1. Baseline instrument and memory meet 90% accuracy and 95% validity separately in every family/cell. These are point-estimate development gates.
2. In **each gain stratum**, information exceeds baseline counterfactual-response probability by at least 10 percentage points in the equal-family pool, and exceeds each of `u`, `q`, and the mean of the three random arms by at least 5 points. Every corresponding family/gain contrast is nonnegative.
3. Every comparison arm has at least 95% validity within each family and loses no more than 5 points relative to its paired baseline. Information intervention loses no more than 5 points of memory accuracy in either family at either sign, with memory accuracy remaining at least 90%. On reinforcement cells, information loses no more than 5 points of instrument accuracy within either family.

These thresholds select a candidate; they are not significance or noninferiority claims. Select dose 0.5 if it passes, regardless of a larger effect at dose 1.0. Otherwise select 1.0 if it passes. If neither passes, stop; do not increase doses, add sites, refit again, or pool failed strata.

## Estimands, multiplicity, and statistical units

The primary outcome is **valid counterfactual response**, using the actual action map. Invalid, capped-without-valid-closure, and reread responses are zero. All assigned rows remain in the denominator. A categorical informative-minus-uninformative margin is descriptive; invalidation can move that margin toward zero and must not count as a counterfactual success.

Primary inference is instrument-only. There are four paired comparisons: primary donor minus baseline, primary donor minus `u` control, primary donor minus `q` control, and primary donor minus the arithmetic mean of the five random controls. Each is evaluated separately for gain zero and gain one, separately for objects and machines and for the equal-family pool: **4 × 2 × 3 = 24 primary endpoints**. The five-random average is computed inside each source/world before resampling. Also report every random arm individually, without claiming five independent replications. Secondary donor route and same-gain donor results are descriptive under this protocol.

Allocate familywise alpha **0.025** to these 24 endpoints. Use 100,000 paired, whole-semantic-world bootstrap draws, stratified by family and its 12 fixed complement/action-mapping strata; retain all cells, arms, readouts and any repeated renderings together. Resample nine worlds with replacement within each stratum, preserving the planned quotas. Each gain stratum has two cells per world, averaged before the world mean. Give the 12 design strata equal weight within family and the two families equal weight in the pool. Use two-sided percentile intervals at confidence `1 − 0.025/24` for each endpoint. Positive mechanism evidence requires every lower bound to exceed zero and each pooled primary-donor-minus-baseline point estimate to be at least 10 points. Report effect sizes and adjusted intervals, not a bootstrap “p = 0.” A degenerate bootstrap is flagged; it alone cannot certify an effect. For a degenerate world contrast, require a conservative bounded-mean lower bound above zero, using range [−1,1] and the corresponding endpoint tail alpha.

The number of calibration candidates is disclosed, but the one chosen candidate is evaluated once on fresh confirmation; the 3,360 descriptive site metrics are not additional confirmatory outcomes. No refitting, additional confirmation look, early efficacy stopping, or data-dependent replacement follows a disappointing endpoint. Report complete world counts and all unavailable endpoints.

If all primary and preservation requirements pass, new/repeat generalization can be tested as a gated secondary family: primary donor minus baseline, two gain strata, two polarities, and three populations gives **12 endpoints**, Bonferroni alpha 0.05 with the same 100,000 paired-world resamples. Do not claim those secondary tests when the primary/preservation gate fails. This is an ordered testing rule, not a way to relabel secondary success as primary success.

### Preservation: exact finite-sample safeguard

Allocate the other familywise alpha **0.025** to exactly six one-sided noninferiority endpoints: target validity, target-memory accuracy, and balanced-count accuracy, each in each family. The maximum tolerated loss is **5 percentage points**, fixed prospectively.

For each endpoint, define a binary harmed-world indicator: one if any relevant treated response newly fails relative to its paired successful baseline in that world; zero otherwise. The exact treated arm sets are:

- **Target validity:** instrument zero sham, self projection, primary donor, secondary donor, same-gain donor, `u`, `q`, and all five random arms; plus zero sham, self projection and primary donor in each of new, repeat and memory. Include every four-cell response. This prevents invalid control responses from silently depressing the control counterfactual-response endpoint.
- **Target-memory accuracy:** zero sham, self projection and primary donor, across all four cells. A same-gain memory arm is not scheduled and is not claimed as tested.
- **Balanced-count accuracy:** zero sham and information +D and −D across all six count/query cells. Count has no scheduled donor or self-projection arm.

Include baseline-failing worlds in the denominator, not only baseline-correct worlds. A validly recorded invalid baseline response is an observed failure, not a missing measurement. Missing or malformed baseline measurements make the endpoint unavailable; incomplete operational coverage independently prevents a scientific pass. A newly invalid competence response is also newly incorrect. This event is conservative: its probability upper-bounds average paired deterioration across the included cells/arms.

Let `a = 0.025/6`, `n` be the number of independent semantic worlds in an endpoint, and `h` its harmed-world count. Fixed design strata can have different harm probabilities, so do not assume a common Bernoulli risk merely to obtain a narrower interval. Use the following one-sided upper bound on their average harm probability for actual noninferiority decisions:

- If `h = 0`, use `U = 1 − a^(1/n)`. This remains valid for independent, nonidentical world risks: `P(no harms) = product(1 − p_i) ≤ (1 − mean(p_i))^n` by the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality. It does not require equal risk in the 12 design strata.
- If `h > 0`, use the conservative independent bounded-variable bound `U = min(1, h/n + sqrt(log(1/a)/(2n)))` (Hoeffding). A Clopper–Pearson interval may also be displayed, explicitly labeled as binomial-exact under independent identically distributed Bernoulli trials; it is not the certification bound for heterogeneous fixed strata.

Noninferiority requires `U` to be **at most 0.05** for every endpoint, consistent with allowing no more than five percentage points of loss. Do not use a zero-variance bootstrap interval to certify preservation. The zero-harm bound is approximately **4.95% at n=108** and **4.46% at n=120**. Thus the proposed sample can establish this conservative 5-point criterion if there are no harmed worlds; any harmed world makes preservation inconclusive at these sample sizes under the specified bound, although actual mean loss can be well below five points. Inconclusive preservation is not evidence of damage. That limited power is intentional and must be stated. Do not widen margins or pool away a failing family after results. Also report ordinary paired mean losses and count 1/2/4 strata descriptively.
